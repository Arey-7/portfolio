I'm a final-year Electronic and Computer Engineering student at Jomo Kenyatta
University of Agriculture and Technology, in Kenya. I build systems for
environments where the infrastructure can't be assumed — unreliable power,
intermittent connectivity, shared bandwidth, and payment rails that look nothing
like a credit card.

That constraint is what I find interesting. Designing for a fast, always-on
network is a solved problem with a thousand tutorials. Designing for four hours
of power a day and a link that disappears without warning forces you to think
about what your system does when its assumptions fail — which, it turns out, is
the only question that matters once anything is deployed.

Most of that thinking has gone into EduAccess, an offline-first learning network
that puts a full content library inside a community with no dependable internet
and lets students pay for access in mobile-money increments. It took 2nd
runner-up at the Engineers Board of Kenya's Engineering Recognition and
Excellence Awards in 2026, and I'm now developing it into a standalone product.

I'm currently building an invoicing system, and looking for backend and systems
work where the hard part is correctness under failure rather than scale.
